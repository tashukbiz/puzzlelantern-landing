How to play

Word games with sentence clues: a complete Word Sense round

17 September 20266 min read· Updated 16 October 2026

Word Sense is a daily word game where you type the word missing from a sentence. Each Daily has three words in easy, medium and hard order. You can ask for up to five clues per word, and a wrong guess spends a clue too.

A sentence can support several plausible words at first. That is part of the puzzle: each extra clue adds a constraint. The complete sample round below uses original sentences and illustrative difficulty labels. It is not a live Daily or a list of today’s answers.

How the five-clue ladder works

The available clue kinds have a fixed order: opposite, synonym, word length, second sentence, first letter, definition. A word with an opposite uses the first five. A word without an opposite skips that rung and ends with the definition instead. Either way, there are five clues, revealed one at a time.

A wrong guess spends one clue, the same cost as asking. Usually it reveals the next hidden rung. If the guess exactly matches a still-hidden synonym or opposite clue, it reveals that matching clue instead, so the game does not later offer your own guess as new information. If all five clues are already spent, another wrong guess ends that word. You may also give up. At the end of a word, the answer card supplies its meaning and related words.

Sample word 1: an everyday adjective

The room became ___ when everyone stopped talking.

“Silent”, “quiet” and “peaceful” could all sound reasonable. Try the rungs in order, stopping whenever you think you know the answer.

Word 1: reveal the five clues and how they help
  1. Opposite: noisy. This points to the sound level, rather than the room’s temperature or brightness.
  2. Synonym: silent. “Peaceful” becomes less exact; we are looking for little or no noise.
  3. Length: 5 letters. SILENT has six letters. QUIET fits the length.
  4. Second sentence: “Please stay ___ while the baby sleeps.” The same adjective must work naturally here too.
  5. First letter: Q. This confirms the start of the intended answer.
Word 1: reveal the answer

QUIET. It fits both sentences, has five letters and begins with Q. The earlier synonym was a clue to its meaning, not the word to enter.

Sample word 2: a hesitant decision

Arun was ___ to lend his new camera to a stranger.

Perhaps he was unwilling, cautious, hesitant or reluctant. The sentence suggests a feeling, but does not by itself fix one exact spelling.

Word 2: reveal the five clues and how they help
  1. Opposite: willing. This rules out an eager agreement.
  2. Synonym: hesitant. The word concerns holding back from a decision, rather than the camera’s value.
  3. Length: 9 letters. HESITANT has eight; RELUCTANT and UNWILLING still merit consideration.
  4. Second sentence: “Even after the invitation, she remained ___ to join the dance.” A candidate must fit this hesitation too.
  5. First letter: R. This separates RELUCTANT from UNWILLING.
Word 2: reveal the answer

RELUCTANT. It describes being unwilling or hesitant to do something. A plausible synonym can still be a wrong game answer when it fails a later constraint.

Sample word 3: something that does not last

The chalk drawing’s beauty was ___; the rain washed it away that afternoon.

“Brief”, “temporary” and “fleeting” are useful starting ideas. This time the intended word is less everyday.

Word 3: reveal the five clues and how they help
  1. Opposite: lasting. The relevant contrast is duration.
  2. Synonym: short-lived. This narrows the meaning further, without supplying the spelling.
  3. Length: 9 letters. BRIEF and FLEETING are too short; TEMPORARY is still possible.
  4. Second sentence: “The mayfly became a symbol of ___ beauty.” The word must again describe something that lasts a short time.
  5. First letter: E. The intended answer begins differently from TEMPORARY.
Word 3: reveal the answer

EPHEMERAL. It means lasting for a short time. The final initial distinguishes it from other words that fit the broad meaning.

What if you guess too soon?

Suppose you type SILENT for word 1 before asking for a clue. In this sample it is not the answer. In the app that miss would spend one clue and reveal the matching synonym clue, “silent”, even though the opposite rung comes first when you ask normally. You would still have four clues left to reveal. You have learned something, but not for free.

A clue is a useful choice when several answers remain plausible. There is no need to treat an early miss as evidence about your vocabulary: it can simply reflect information the puzzle had not yet supplied.

How the score works, including the clock

Completion comes first in the ranking. Among completed runs, solved tiers carry weights of 1 for easy, 2 for medium and 4 for hard, followed by clues saved as the tiebreaker. Thus solving the hard word carries more tier weight than easy and medium together. Equal rank keys are ordered by submission time.

A visible elapsed clock is present, but elapsed time is not part of the ranking. Do not confuse “no speed score” with “no clock”. The timer comparison separates those preferences across several games.

What to play next

Word Sense draws its words and clue information from a maintained English lexicon. The next word opens on its first action, so you can pause at an answer card. It is Daily only, with no More puzzles mode. Sudoku, Recall and Vanish offer additional rounds in the same app.

For more examples here, try ten original clue puzzles with answers. The Word Sense product guide covers getting started in Puzzle Lantern on iPhone and iPad. The game practises solving these particular words; it does not establish broader changes in language ability.
