Word games without timers: five choices and their clock rules

16 October 20266 min read

If you want a word game without a countdown, start with 7 Little Words, Daily Waffle or Wordiply. If you also want no visible clock, be more selective: Word Sense shows elapsed time even though speed does not affect its ranking. Alphaguess has no visible clock during the play screen we inspected, but reports time in its result display.

There are three different preferences here: no deadline to make a move, no clock on screen, and no reward for playing faster. This comparison separates them instead of calling all three “untimed”. Puzzle Lantern is our own app. The other entries are based on official rules and limited browser inspection on 16 October 2026, not full app testing.

7 Little Words: solve written clues

Read a clue and combine letter-group tiles into its answer. The supplied pieces give you another way in when a definition alone does not help. Solving one clue also removes tiles from consideration for the others. The official web game showed a daily puzzle selector and hints, with no clock on the board.

Web play is ad-supported. The iPhone/iPad app is free to download and sells subscriptions and individual puzzle packs; free access is not the entire catalog. Choose this if you like clues more than open-ended word finding.

Daily Waffle: work within a move budget

Waffle gives you letters to swap into intersecting words. You have 15 swaps, and the remaining swaps determine stars. There is still a constraint, but it is a move budget you can think about, not a deadline.

Use the Daily game for this experience. Waffle’s separate Challenge mode is timed, so the whole site cannot be described as timer-free. The daily browser game is available with ads; supporter access removes ads and includes extras such as Waffle Royale. Some archive access has ad gates.

Wordiply: search for a longer word

The Guardian’s Wordiply asks for words containing a supplied starter sequence. You get five goes. Its rules score your longest word against the target length and count the total letters used. The inspected browser game opened without payment and showed no play clock.

For an invented example, a starter of art could appear inside carton or earth. The sequence must stay together and in order. This is a word-finding task rather than a written-clue puzzle, and five accepted guesses provide a stopping point.

Alphaguess: use alphabetical feedback

In Alphaguess, each guess tells you whether the hidden word comes before or after it alphabetically. That narrows a dictionary interval rather than supplying a definition. There was no visible countdown or elapsed clock during our limited play-screen check, but its results include time, so avoid it if any recorded timing bothers you.

The browser game has ads and offers paid ad removal. The public page displayed monthly and annual offers; check the checkout’s currency and terms if considering them. We did not buy access or verify a speed leaderboard.

Word Sense: no speed score, but a clock remains

Our Word Sense asks for three words from sentence prompts, with up to five clues per word. Wrong guesses spend clues too. Ranking considers completion, solved difficulty tiers and clues saved. A slower run with the same results receives the same rank key; submission order resolves ties.

It fits “no countdown”, but not “I never want to see a clock”. Puzzle Lantern is on iPhone and iPad, with Android in a closed test. It is free to download, with seven days of initial access in the current configuration and subscription access afterwards; check the app’s offer. Word Sense is Daily only.
