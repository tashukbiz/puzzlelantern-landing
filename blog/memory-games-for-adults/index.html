Memory games for adults: 6 challenges to try

16 October 20267 min read

To try a memory game, first decide what you want to remember: where something was, which objects appeared, or the order of a short sequence. These six activities cover different tasks and need only paper, a few household objects or ordinary playing cards. Each sample is original to this guide.

Take as long as you want to study the examples. Close the “View or hide” panel before answering, or cover it with paper. Check your response once, then either stop or make a fresh round. Repeating the same example is allowed, but you will already know its contents.

1. Rebuild a small pattern

Draw two empty 3×3 grids. Mark three cells on the first, study it, cover it, then copy the marked positions onto the second. You are remembering locations, not the order in which the marks were drawn.

View or hide the example

Recall: Which three cells were marked? Rows run from top to bottom and columns from left to right.

Reveal answer and explanation

R1C1, R2C1 and R2C2. Together they make a small L in the upper-left part of the grid. A rotated L would be a different answer because position matters.

For a new round, move one mark. Change the pattern size only if you want more to remember.

2. Remember an object tray

Put four safe, ordinary objects on a tray. Look at them, cover the tray with a cloth and list the objects in any order. For solo play, prepare the tray yourself; with a partner, let them choose it.

View or hide the example

Sample tray: a teaspoon, a key, a spool of thread and a lemon.

Recall: Name all four objects. Their positions do not matter in this version.

Reveal answer and explanation

Teaspoon, key, spool of thread, lemon. “Spoon” and “thread spool” are fine descriptions of the same objects. This round asks for identities; it does not ask for colours or left-to-right order.

3. Identify a missing card

Use four different cards. Study the hand, turn away while a partner removes one, then identify what is missing. A solo version works with the covered example below.

View or hide the example

Original hand: 2 of hearts, 6 of clubs, 9 of diamonds, king of spades.

Recall: The remaining cards are king of spades, 2 of hearts and 9 of diamonds. Which card disappeared?

Reveal answer and explanation

6 of clubs. Each remaining card matches one original identity. Rearranging them does not change the answer. In a larger hand, remember both rank and suit: 6 of clubs and 6 of hearts are different cards.

4. Find matching pairs

Take six cards: two aces, two twos and two threes. Define a match as the same rank, regardless of suit. Shuffle them face down in two rows of three. Turn over two per turn, keep a matching pair face up, and turn a mismatch face down again.

Sample turn: You saw an ace at top left and a three at bottom middle, then covered both. On your next turn you reveal an ace at top right. Where should you look for its partner?

Reveal answer and explanation

Top left. You are connecting a card identity with a location seen on a previous turn. Unlike the missing-card activity, this game asks where a matching card is hiding. Finish when all three pairs are exposed.

5. Repeat an action sequence

A partner performs three simple actions once. Repeat them in the same order after they stop. Use hand gestures instead of sounds if that suits you, and agree on the actions before starting.

View or hide the example

Sample sequence: clap, touch the table, clap, raise one hand.

Recall: Reproduce the four actions in order.

Reveal answer and explanation

Clap → touch the table → clap → raise one hand. There are four actions even though clap appears twice. Naming all the action types would not preserve the sequence.

6. Recall a short word list

Write five familiar nouns, read them once at your chosen pace, then hide them and write down as many as you remember. Agree in advance whether order counts. Here it does not.

View or hide the example

Harbour, candle, jacket, pear, violin.

Reveal answer and explanation

Harbour, candle, jacket, pear and violin, in any order. If you remember “boat” instead of harbour, it is an association rather than the original word. That distinction helps you check the task fairly without treating the result as a personal assessment.

Which format suits you?

Choose patterns for positions, trays for objects, pairs for identity-plus-location, and action sequences for order. Adjust one thing at a time: add an object, enlarge the grid or lengthen the sequence. There is no need to race or assign yourself a memory score.

If you prefer prepared rounds, Recall asks you to reproduce highlighted grid positions and Vanish asks which card disappeared from a hand. Both Puzzle Lantern games have timed previews, a visible elapsed clock and time as a final ranking tie-breaker. They do not offer the tray, sequence or word-list activities above. Choose the paper versions if you want complete control over viewing time.
